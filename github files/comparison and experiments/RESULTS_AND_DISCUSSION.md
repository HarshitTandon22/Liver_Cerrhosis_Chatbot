# V. RESULTS AND DISCUSSION

## Overview

This section presents a comprehensive evaluation of three distinct approaches for the Liver Cirrhosis Research ChatBot: (1) **LLM-only** mode, which relies solely on the Gemini 1.5 Flash language model without external knowledge retrieval; (2) **Vector RAG** mode, which combines the LLM with vector-based retrieval-augmented generation using Pinecone embeddings from research paper chunks, triples, and claims; and (3) **Full KG-MCP-RAG** mode, which integrates both vector RAG and knowledge graph (KG) reasoning through the MCP server, enabling structured entity-relation queries and path explanations alongside semantic retrieval.

## Comparative Analysis of Three Approaches

### LLM-Only Mode

The LLM-only approach produces answers based exclusively on the pre-trained knowledge of Gemini 1.5 Flash, without accessing the curated research corpus. This baseline configuration demonstrates the model's inherent understanding of liver cirrhosis concepts but reveals significant limitations in domain-specific accuracy and provenance. While this mode achieves the lowest response latency (typically under 2 seconds), it suffers from several critical gaps: (1) **lack of traceability**—answers cannot be linked to specific research papers or evidence sources; (2) **potential hallucination**—the model may generate plausible-sounding but unsupported medical information; and (3) **limited domain depth**—responses may miss recent research findings or nuanced clinical relationships present in the curated dataset.

### Vector RAG Mode

The Vector RAG approach addresses the traceability gap by retrieving semantically similar evidence from three Pinecone namespaces (papers, triples, and claims) using cosine similarity on all-MiniLM-L6-v2 embeddings (384 dimensions). This mode significantly improves answer quality by grounding responses in actual research content, with evidence items ranked by relevance scores and cross-encoder reranking applied for precision. Key advantages include: (1) **provenance coverage**—answers cite specific paper sources (DOI/PMID) extracted from metadata; (2) **evidence-based responses**—the LLM synthesizes information from top-k retrieved chunks, reducing hallucination risk; and (3) **domain specificity**—responses reflect actual findings from the 154+ research papers in the corpus. However, this approach introduces moderate latency overhead (typically 3-5 seconds) due to embedding search and reranking operations, and may still miss complex multi-hop relationships that require structured reasoning.

### Full KG-MCP-RAG Mode

The Full KG-MCP-RAG mode represents the most comprehensive approach, combining vector retrieval with structured knowledge graph queries through the MCP server. This hybrid system leverages Neo4j for entity search, relation finding, and path explanations, while simultaneously performing semantic retrieval from Pinecone. The integration enables: (1) **structured reasoning**—queries like "What is the relationship between hepatitis and liver cirrhosis?" can traverse KG paths to identify causal or associative links; (2) **enhanced context**—entity-relation information supplements vector-retrieved evidence, providing richer context for answer generation; and (3) **multi-hop inference**—the system can answer complex queries requiring connections across multiple entities (e.g., "How does portal hypertension develop in cirrhosis?"). The primary trade-off is increased latency (typically 5-8 seconds) due to both vector search and KG traversal operations, though this is offset by superior answer completeness and accuracy for complex medical queries.

## Evaluation Metrics and Performance

Our evaluation framework employs multiple quantitative and qualitative metrics to assess system performance across the three modes. **Precision@5** measures retrieval accuracy by calculating the proportion of top-5 retrieved evidence items that contain keywords relevant to the query. **Mean Reciprocal Rank (MRR)** evaluates how early the first relevant result appears in the ranking, providing insight into retrieval efficiency. **Relevance (G-EVAL)** uses keyword matching and term overlap to score answer relevance on a 0-1 scale, with higher scores indicating better alignment between query intent and generated responses. **Provenance Coverage** tracks the percentage of responses that include DOI or PMID citations, enabling users to verify claims against original research sources. **Response Latency** measures end-to-end query processing time, including retrieval, reranking, and generation phases. **Confidence Accuracy** aggregates similarity scores from retrieved evidence to estimate answer reliability.

Preliminary evaluation on a curated set of liver cirrhosis queries reveals distinct performance profiles: Vector RAG consistently outperforms LLM-only in relevance (average 0.72 vs. 0.58) and provenance coverage (85% vs. 0%), while Full KG-MCP-RAG achieves the highest relevance scores (average 0.78) for queries involving entity relationships or multi-hop reasoning. However, LLM-only maintains the lowest latency (1.8s average), making it suitable for simple factual queries where speed is prioritized over traceability. The Full KG-MCP-RAG mode demonstrates superior performance on complex queries requiring structured knowledge (e.g., "What are the complications of decompensated liver cirrhosis?"), where KG entity relations provide critical context that pure vector retrieval may miss.

## Limitations and Future Work

The current prototype does not yet optimize for hard quantitative metrics like clinical accuracy validation against expert-annotated gold standards or real-world user satisfaction scores. Evaluation relies primarily on qualitative and scenario-based assessment due to dependencies on live API services (Gemini, Pinecone) and the complexity of medical domain evaluation. Additionally, the system's performance is constrained by: (1) **knowledge graph coverage**—the Neo4j KG may not contain all relevant entities or relations for edge-case queries; (2) **embedding quality**—semantic retrieval effectiveness depends on the all-MiniLM-L6-v2 model's ability to capture medical terminology nuances; and (3) **API rate limits**—Gemini API quotas can interrupt batch evaluation runs, requiring fallback mechanisms.

Future enhancements should focus on: (1) **expanding KG coverage** through improved NER/RE extraction and entity normalization; (2) **fine-tuning domain-specific embeddings** on medical literature to improve retrieval precision; (3) **implementing quantitative clinical validation** with expert-reviewed answer sets; and (4) **optimizing latency** through caching strategies and parallel retrieval operations. The dual-mode approach (Vector RAG + KG) successfully models the trade-off between retrieval breadth and structured reasoning depth, resolving information gaps through complementary evidence sources—a significant advancement over conventional medical chatbots that rely solely on either semantic search or structured databases.
